Attack & Defend

A four-player shedding game for one human and three CPU opponents. Defend attacks, add matching ranks, preserve your power cards, and be the first player or team to empty a hand.

How to Play

The goal is simple: get rid of every card in your hand. House Rules can modify exactly when an empty hand wins. In Team Play, either partner going out wins for the team. You play against three CPU players, and the current House Rules determine the starting and replenishment hand sizes.

1. Find the Power suit

At the start of the game, one card is exposed at the bottom of the draw deck. Its suit becomes the Power suit for the entire game. The current Power suit is also shown clearly at the top of the screen.

Power cards can beat non-Power attacks regardless of suit. A Power attack can only be beaten by a higher Power card.

2. Attacking

When it is your turn to attack, select a card and press Play Selected. If you have several cards of the same rank, select one of them and press Select Same Rank to select all legal copies at once. You may start with multiple cards only when they are the same rank and the defender has enough cards to receive them.

Example: if you hold 7♣, 7♦ and 7♥, you can select one 7, press Select Same Rank, then throw all three together if the defender has enough cards to receive them.

3. Joining an attack

Once an attack has started, every player may add cards whose rank already appears on the table. That includes ranks introduced by cards the defender used to cover attacks.

A 7 is attacked and defended with a 10. From then on, additional 7s or 10s may be thrown in, up to the number of cards the defender had available at the start of the battle.

4. Defending

To defend a card, play a higher card of the same suit, or use a Power card. You cannot defend with an unrelated non-Power suit. Defense cards may be freely rearranged or returned to your hand before you finish.

Against 6♥, 9♥ works. If Spades are Power, any ♠ works. If you first defend with a Jack, Jacks become attackable. If you later replace it with an 8♠ Power card, Jacks and 8s are now attackable for the rest of that battle.

5. Taking the table

If you cannot — or do not want to — defend everything, press Take Cards. You pick up every attack and defense card currently on the table.

6. Passing your attack window

When you have nothing else you want to add right now, press Done Adding — For Now. This only passes on the ranks currently available. The CPUs then continue resolving the battle. If a defense card introduces a new rank that matches a card in your hand, you get another opportunity to throw it in. The battle ends only when everything is covered and nobody has another legal card to add.

Example: you attack with a 6 and pass. CPU 3 covers it with a Jack. If you hold a Jack, the game returns to you and lets you add that Jack before the battle can end.

7. Replenishing your hand

After each battle, players draw back toward the configured hand size while cards remain in the deck. The player who initiated that attack draws first, followed by the others in the established attack direction.

8. First player & attack direction

A player is randomly selected to attack first. That player chooses clockwise or counter-clockwise play before the first attack. If you are selected, the game will ask you to choose. The chosen direction remains fixed for the entire game.

9. Free For All vs. Team Play

Free For All: every player is on their own. Team Play: opposite seats are partners, so you and CPU 2 are one team while CPU 1 and CPU 3 are the other. After the draw pile is exhausted, if either member of a team gets rid of all their cards, that entire team wins. You are still technically allowed to attack CPU 2, but the game will visually warn you because attacking your own partner is usually a strategic exception rather than the normal choice.

Quick controls

Select Same Rank quickly selects every legal card in your hand matching the rank of your current selection, up to the number of cards the defender can receive. Play Selected plays your chosen card(s). Done Adding — For Now passes your current opportunity; a newly introduced defense rank can give you another one. Take Cards gives up the defense and takes the whole battle. When defending, you can select the attack card you want to cover before playing your defense card. Defense cards are not locked: click any defense you have already played to return it to your hand and rearrange it. However, every defense rank you reveal stays eligible for throw-ins for the rest of that battle, even if you take that card back. Once every attack is covered exactly how you want, press Defense Complete.
